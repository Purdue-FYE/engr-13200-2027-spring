4.1.2. PCA: Engineering Calculations in MATLAB#

In this activity, you will complete a MATLAB script used to perform an engineering calculation. You will complete missing calculations and communicate the final results using formatted text output.

Required Files#

Download the MATLAB Script Template and save it as username_4_1_2.m.

Using the MATLAB Template

Rename your file(s) with your Purdue username by replacing “template” in the filename. Then fill out any required information in the template, such as your name, email, and course details.

Instructions#

A cylindrical water storage tank is being installed as part of a water supply system. The tank has a diameter of \(2.4 m\) and a height of \(3.5 m\). A pump supplies water to the tank at a constant flow rate of \(85 L/\text{min}\).

The volume of a cylinder is calculated using:

\[ V = \pi r^2h \]

where:

  • \(V\) is the volume of the tank in \(m^3\)

  • \(r\) is the radius of the tank in \(m\)

  • \(h\) is the height of the tank in \(m\)

The time required to fill the tank can be calculated using:

\[ t = \frac{V}{Q} \]

where:

  • \(t\) is the fill time in \(\text{min}\)

  • \(V\) is the tank capacity in \(L\)

  • \(Q\) is the pump flow rate in \(L/\text{min}\)

You will write a script to determine the capacity of the tank in liters and the time required for the pump to completely fill the empty tank in minutes.

Initialization#

Create descriptive variables to store the tank diameter and tank height. Use the input function to prompt the user to enter the pump flow rate in L/min. Include the appropriate units in comments describing the initialized variables.

Calculations#

Calculate the capacity of the tank in liters and the time required to completely fill the tank.

  • Store all intermediate and final calculated quantities in descriptive variables.

  • Suppress the output of all initialization and calculation commands so that intermediate values are not displayed in the Command Window.

Formatted Text and Figure Displays#

Use fprintf to display the tank capacity and fill time exactly as shown in the Sample Output below.

  • Use the calculated variables in your fprintf statement. Do not hardcode the calculated numerical results.

  • Display each numerical result to two decimal places.

  • Ensure that only the required formatted output is displayed in the Command Window when the script is run.
